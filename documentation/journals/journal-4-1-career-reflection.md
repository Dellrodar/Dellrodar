# Journal 4-1: Career Reflection and Status Checkpoints

**Emilio Crocco**  
CS-499: Computer Science Capstone  
Southern New Hampshire University  
July 25, 2026

When I entered the Computer Science program, my primary goal was to strengthen my development knowledge and continue building a long-term career in software engineering. That overall direction has not changed because I have continued gaining professional experience as a full-stack software engineer while completing the program. However, my understanding of the career has expanded considerably. I originally viewed software engineering mainly as writing functional code, but I now recognize that it also requires architecture, security, testing, documentation, data management, maintainability, and communication with technical and nontechnical stakeholders. Modernizing the Grazioso Salvare application has reinforced this perspective because I must consider how the frontend, backend, authorization controls, algorithms, and database structures operate as one system. Career research has also supported my decision to remain in this field. Employment opportunities for software developers are expected to continue growing as organizations increase their reliance on artificial intelligence, automation, cybersecurity, and connected software systems (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). After completing my undergraduate degree, I may pursue a certification in cloud architecture or security that complements my professional responsibilities. I have also considered an advanced degree, although I would first evaluate whether it would directly support my future career goals.

At this point, I have made meaningful progress toward all five course outcomes, although some will be demonstrated more completely in the final ePortfolio. My narratives, documentation, and code review demonstrate outcome two by communicating technical decisions to different audiences. The architectural redesign and rescue-animal matching system demonstrate outcome three through the evaluation of design alternatives, algorithmic principles, performance, and technical trade-offs. Implementing the FastAPI and React architecture, role-based authorization, database-side scoring, deterministic ranking, pagination, and automated tests supports outcome four. Authentication, permission enforcement, input validation, and bounded pagination demonstrate outcome five. I have also developed outcome one by incorporating instructor feedback and adjusting my development process. In response to the feedback from my previous journal, I completed the algorithms and data structures enhancement through seven focused commits in pull request 3 rather than uploading all changes at once. These commits separately show the development of the animal data model, authenticated search, rescue profiles, matching algorithm, frontend search interface, ranked results, tests, and documentation. The database enhancement remains the next major stage, and I will continue using focused commits and clearly documenting which files are modified during each step.
